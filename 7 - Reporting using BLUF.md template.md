# INTEL WRITING CHEAT SHEET (BLUF-Driven, Decision-Ready)

## 0) BLUF — Lead With the Answer
- **First sentence = main judgment + so-what + action** (one screen, ~2–3 lines). :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}  
- Don’t bury the judgment; average attention on a report is ~**26 seconds**. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  
- Formula: **We assess that [X will happen] [when/where/timeframe] because [key drivers]. So what: [impact to audience].** :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

---

## 1) North Star (PIR vs KIQ) — When to Use What
**Assessment:** Use **PIR** to anchor decision-need and prioritization; translate each PIR into a precise **KIQ** to focus analysis and writing.  
- **If the task is decision-driven and audience-defined** (executive needs, operational tasks): lead with **PIR** (what the decision-maker cares about). *(Best practice / inference)*  
- **If the task is analytic-focus and scoping** (what you must answer to satisfy the PIR): craft a sharp **KIQ** and answer it explicitly in the BLUF and body. The document frames **KIQ as your “North Star.”** :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}  
**Bottom line:** **PIR → KIQ → BLUF.** Keep PIR as the driver for “So What,” use KIQ to discipline the write-up. *(Inference aligned to document guidance on KIQ centrality.)* :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

---

## 2) So What — Make Relevance Unmissable
- Ask: **Why does this matter to this audience right now?** :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}  
- State **consequences**, **options**, **recommended next step** in the BLUF/first section. :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

---

## 3) Estimative Language — Say How Sure You Are
- Use calibrated terms (e.g., **likely, very likely, unlikely**) and keep them consistent with ODNI/ICD-203 style guidance. :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}  
- Pair words with **drivers of confidence** (source quality, corroboration, analytic leverage).

---

## 4) Credibility — Show Your Work (Briefly)
- One line on **basis of assessment**: sources, methods, vantage point. :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}  
- Example: “**Based on analyst observations, threat-forum collection, and primary interviews, we assess…**” :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}

---

## 5) Key Uncertainties & Assumptions — Be Explicit
- List **unknowns that could flip the judgment** and **assumptions you rely on**. :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}  
- Flag **decision points** where new info would change the call.

---

## 6) Timeframe — Bound Your Judgment
- Answer **when** and **how long it holds**; identify **review triggers**. :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

---

## 7) Implications for Operations/Policy — Convert Insight to Action
- Spell out **operational impacts**, **policy considerations**, and **what to monitor**. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}

---

## 8) Avoid Non-Analysis — Add Value or Cut It
- Ditch vague, backward-looking filler (e.g., “based on past events, this will continue”). Provide **forward-leaning** judgments or remove. :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}

---

## 9) Fast Workflow (PIR→KIQ→BLUF)
1) **Confirm PIR(s)** with owner (decision, timing, tolerance). *(Inference)*  
2) Convert each PIR into a **sharp KIQ** (one question you can answer). :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}  
3) Draft **BLUF** that answers the KIQ and lands the **So What**. :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}  
4) Add **estimative language** + **credibility line**. :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}  
5) List **uncertainties/assumptions** + **timeframe**. :contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}  
6) Close with **implications & next actions**; cut non-analysis. :contentReference[oaicite:18]{index=18}

---
